Hi there, My name is Yutong Wu 雨潼 (/yoo-tong/).

I'm a neuroengineer in practice and a natural scientist at heart. My background is in computational cognitive science and biology at Wellesley College, with research and coursework at MIT and the Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics.

I'm grateful to my training in biology, for showing me the wondrous world of how organisms and their societies actually work; to computer science, for teaching me how to formalize and build; and to philosophy, for giving me an abstract and reflective foundation for the nature of my inquiry and creation.

I'm currently a development engineer at a BCI startup focused on building an sEEG-based brain foundation model. My work focuses on

  1. building a fully non-invasive interface ecosystem (sEMG, EEG, eye tracking, force feedback, and physiological health signals) for applications in assistive technology and human-robot interaction, and
  2. researching a brain foundation model that learns transferable neural representations to decode human intent and enable adaptive interaction.

Two long-term questions I'm interested in are:

  1. How do biological systems perceive and understand the physical world, and how can engineered systems or technology be inspired by such discoveries?
  2. How should technology be designed and applied to augment a person's cognition and abilities over the long term, in a shape the nervous system is already used to?

Outside the lab, I enjoy photography, learning languages, playing music (Guzheng), and theater.
